A streak counts how long you've kept going, and makes missing one day feel like losing everything. Jamaal doesn't count them. It shows you a grid of days instead, and a quiet week is just a lighter-coloured row.

## Four kinds of habit

When you make a habit, you choose what question it asks each day:

- **Did I do it?** Done or not. Good for things like taking a vitamin or making the bed.
- **Did I do it a number of times?** A counted habit: glasses of water, pages, rounds of dhikr. You tap to add one.
- **Did I do it for a number of minutes?** A timed habit, like reading or a run. You can start a timer, or add the minutes by hand if you did it without your phone.
- **Did I avoid it?** For something you're trying not to do. You only log the day you slipped, and nothing else.

A habit can have presets to start from, such as Qur'an reading, or you can begin with a blank one.

## Times of day

A habit doesn't have to happen once a day. Give it a few times of day, such as morning and evening, each with its own hours, and, if you like, its own reminder. Each one appears on Today when it's due.

## The grid

Open a habit and you'll see its days as a grid. Darker squares are days you did more of it; empty ones are quiet days. There is no "streak" to protect, and a day without it is shown in a soft colour, never as an error.

## Groups, and weekly targets

Habits can sit in a group, like Morning, which appears on Today as a pill. And a habit can aim for a number of times a week, such as running three times, instead of every day. It stays on Today until the week's goal is met.

## When life gets in the way

You can pause a habit, with a reason: a trip, an illness, a hard season. A paused habit asks nothing of you and doesn't count against you. Pick it up again when you're ready.

## Where it shows up

Habits you've done feed the Wellbeing page in words, such as "most days", and never a figure to chase. A timed habit uses the same focus timer you'd use for a task, so the minutes land in the right place.
