Derived from Jamaal Screens v3 · nothing else

ThreadsKit
measured, not declared.

Every value below was counted out of the thirty-four drawn screens in Jamaal Screens v3 — what the rows, chips, bezels and density grids actually use, across every inline style in the file. No external palette, no inherited scale. The screens are the reference; this is their transcript.

Jamaal is built on it first. Riqa and Hashiya then inherit the same contract — same type roles, same spacing, same radii, same elevation — and override colour only. Section 06 draws that line precisely.

Status · the one genuine gap

Three of the four already exist under other names

v3 draws no success, warning, error or info — and mostly that is correct rather than missing, because the palette already carries those meanings in its working colours. Importing a generic red/amber/green set would put four foreign hues into a two-accent system and undo the calm the screens are built on.

success → accent
Sage already means settled — the completed row, the logged prayer, the filled density cell. A second green would be the same idea twice.
warning → terra
Terracotta is already the colour of a decision being asked for. The overloaded capacity meter and a closing soon ritual window are exactly that, so they inherit it rather than earning an amber.
lapsed → missed
A closed window or a skipped habit is not an error — nothing went wrong, a day just passed. That distinction is the product's whole temperament, and missed protects it.
info → ink2
Nothing in Jamaal announces itself informationally. Plain body ink on card is the treatment; a blue info tint would also be invisible against a blue app.
Needs adding · one hue, two names
There is no colour for refused or destructive
This one is real, and it comes from decisions made after v3 was drawn: Drop on the interruption sheet, deleting a task, and the medication ritual that must refuse a second dose rather than accept it quietly. None of those can borrow terracotta — terracotta is the primary action, so a Drop button in terracotta reads as the thing to press.
So: one new hue, kept in the family by pushing the existing terracotta deeper and redder rather than importing a signal red.
alert
A32E22 / F09A90
alertSoft
F2D4CE / 5A2A22

Note the treatment as much as the hue: destructive is a soft fill with alert text, never a filled alert button. Only terracotta gets to be filled, so the eye still lands on the safe choice first — and the three-way settle sheet stays readable at a glance.
For the shared contract this means ThreadsPalette declares nineteen names, and the four status roles are computed properties with defaults — Jamaal aliases three of them to its working colours, while Riqa, which genuinely has failed uploads and processing states, overrides them with real values. Same contract, no foreign hues forced on the products that don't need them.

The one real problem
These are mockup sizes
A 10 px label used 98 times and 14–16 px body read fine in a 320 px-wide drawing on a large screen. Shipped as 10 pt and 15 pt on a real phone they are too small, and they ignore the user's text-size setting — unacceptable for an app whose whole subject is attention.
Resolve it by treating the roles as ratios, not points: anchor body to the system body text style and derive the rest, so the relationships in the drawings survive while the absolute sizes follow Dynamic Type.
.font(.custom("HankenGrotesk-Regular", size: 17, relativeTo: .body))

One correction from section 15: the gutter is a minimum, not a constant — safe areas and layout margins are often asymmetric on Duo, so each edge resolves independently against its own inset rather than taking a flat 26.

phone · 320 × 693

Wide layouts are a 340 px list beside a detail pane, never a denser grid — the sidebar width is constant across every wide screen drawn: width buys focus, not density. Note these are frames v3 happened to draw, not breakpoints — section 15 replaces them with two size classes, since the real devices resize continuously.
Hit targets
Drawn checkboxes are 20–22 px and chips are 9–12 px tall in padding terms — again, composition sizes. In the build every one of these needs 44 pt minimum, which means the visual mark stays 22 pt and gains an invisible contentShape around it. Worth a token: .hit = 44.
04

Elevation & motion

Two levels, both blue-black
lifty18 r40 s−26 · α.42
floaty10 r26 s−14 · α.50
hair0 1 0 · line
Tinted rgba(6,32,46,·) — cool, deep, and tightened by a negative spread that SwiftUI's .shadow cannot express. Reproduce by shadowing an inset shape behind the card, and wrap it once as a modifier so no call site improvises.
Dark is not a mirror
Shadows lose the blue. Dark uses neutral rgba(0,0,0,.70) at y22 r50 s−28 — deeper, wider, and a different hue. So elevation is four values, not two.
Glass inverts its material. White at 62% over blue in light; white at 9% in dark. One system material will not give you both — carry the alphas as tokens and let the blur be constant. Note this applies to glass only; selected is opaque in both appearances.
Distinct names converge. In dark, terracotta and the rule colour are both #DDA080; sage and density-3 are both #8FCBB8. Keep the names separate even where the hex repeats, or you lose the ability to pull them apart later.
Motion · an honest gap
The screens are still frames: there is no motion in v3 to measure. Rather than invent a curve, derive it from the two behaviours already decided — the ambient timer chip and the complete/defer sheets — and fix the tokens when those are drawn.
Until then the package should expose one duration and one curve as placeholders, clearly marked provisional, so views are not littered with literals that later need finding.

    └── Resources/

A palette is a conformance, not a copy: twenty-one names declared once in ThreadsPalette, and each product supplies values for all of them. That is what makes Riqa cost a file rather than a fork — and what makes a missing token a compile error instead of a wrong colour on screen.
Revised · one target, not four
An earlier draft of this page gave Jamaal, Riqa and Hashiya a target each. That was structure invented ahead of content, and wrong for three reasons. Only one palette exists — the other two would be empty files named after intentions. The module boundary buys nothing, because each app links exactly one palette, and separate targets are only worth it when a consumer needs to link a subset. And three targets invite divergence: the first token added to one and not the others is how a shared contract quietly stops being shared.
Write Riqa's when Riqa has a screen
A palette is 21 colours in one file plus an asset catalog — a morning's work whenever it is genuinely needed, and impossible to get right before then. It is the same argument as not specifying glass from prose: every value in this document is measured from something drawn, and Riqa has nothing drawn yet.
What the protocol still earns
Keep it even with one conformance, for one reason: it makes a missing token a compile error, which is precisely the failure that let v3 drift from the system in the first place. If Riqa and Hashiya never materialise, the protocol was cheap insurance. Add a second product only when a real consumer needs to link without the others.
06

What Riqa and Hashiya inherit

Inherited · unchanged
Type, space, radius, elevation
The seven roles and three leadings, the 26 gutter and 14 gap, the four radii with the pill dominant, and the two-level tinted shadow. A Riqa screen should be recognisable as the same hand as a Jamaal screen with the colour removed — that is the whole point of deriving from one reference.
Overridden · colour only
Twenty-one values, one file
Ground, ink, two accents with their pressed tints and their onAccent, alert, the density ramp, line, glass and selected — plus any status role the product actually needs. Keep the roles honest when you re-hue: one warm accent for the action being asked for, one cool for what is settled. A product that gives itself three accents has left the system.
Product-only · never shared
The density ramp's meaning
Jamaal's three steps encode "density, not streaks". Riqa's equivalent will encode something else entirely — credits, or file state. Share the shape (three steps plus a negative) and let each product name what the steps mean.
Before any of this is code
Both of the questions that used to sit here are now answered — the type scale is rebased in section 08 and the contrast is measured in section 09. What follows in 07–13 is specification, not discussion: values a build can be driven from directly.
07

What a system still needs

Ten gaps · in the order they bite

Sections 01–04 are the static half: what a thing looks like sitting still. A design system is mostly the other half — what a thing looks like being touched, being empty, being wrong, or being read right-to-left. None of that is in v3, because still frames cannot show it.

Blocking · needed before the first screen is built
01 · interaction states
Every component has four, v3 draws one
Rest, pressed, focused, disabled. Pressed matters most on iOS — and the answer cannot be the web's translateY(-1px) lift, because a finger is already covering the element. Propose: fill darkens one step, no movement, and disabled is opacity only so no new colour is needed.
Rest Pressed Focus Off
02 · the empty day
The most important screen isn't drawn
An app called "one list, today only" spends real time with nothing on the list — first launch, and every morning before capture. v3 has no empty state anywhere, so there is no token for the ground a nearly-blank screen sits on, and no decision about whether emptiness reads as calm or as failure. It must read as calm.
Needs: an empty treatment per surface (today, habits, rituals, history), and a rule that an empty list is never a call to action.
03 · icons
Not one is specified
Stroke weight, corner join, size, and optical alignment to the mono label are all unset — and icons will appear on every row, chip and tab bar. Set three values and it never comes up again: 22 pt box, 1.75 pt stroke, round joins, tinted with the ink ramp and never with an accent unless it is the accent's meaning.
Needed early · cheap now, expensive later
04 · rtl & numerals
An app with Salah in it will be read right-to-left
Free if you never write .leading as .left, and painful to retrofit. Two extra calls: the density grid must fill from the trailing edge, and Arabic-Indic numerals need to be a supported rendering of times and counts, not a find-and-replace later.
05 · component inventory
Tokens aren't a system yet
v3 redraws the same row, chip, sheet and density grid across 34 screens, and nothing names them. Until they are named they will drift. The real inventory is small — task row, chip, pill button, settle sheet, density grid, window bar, capacity meter, section header — eight things, and every screen is a composition of them.
06 · motion & haptics
The completion moment is undesigned
Section 04 notes there is no motion to measure, but the gap is bigger than a curve: ticking something off is the app's one reward, and in a product with no streaks it is the only feedback there is. That makes it a token-level decision — one duration, one curve, and one haptic weight per event class (complete, defer, drop, window closing).
07 · voice & copy
"Nothing nags" is a copy rule, unwritten
The strings in v3 are good and accidental. Write the rules down or they erode: no exclamation marks, no praise, no guilt, never second person imperative about the past. "Three of five" not "You missed two!". This is the cheapest section to write and the one most likely to be violated by whoever writes the next screen.
Later · but write down that they're deferred
08 · off-app surfaces
The ambient chip already lives on the lock screen, so the system has a home-screen widget and a Live Activity whether it plans for them or not. Both ignore your palette's ground and inherit the wallpaper — which means glass and the ink ramp need a variant that works on an unknown background. Nothing else in the system has that problem.
09 · elevation on dark grounds. A card on deep cannot use a shadow — it is already dark. v3 solves it with glass, but that is undocumented as a rule: on dark, elevation is material, not shadow.
10 · versioning. Three products will consume this. Decide now whether a token can ever be removed, or only deprecated — otherwise the first Riqa release makes the answer for you.
My recommendation
Gaps 01–06 and 09 are specified in sections 08–13 below, with real values. 07, 08 and 10 — voice, off-app surfaces, versioning — stay open deliberately: they are decisions about the product and the org, not the package, and nothing in the build is blocked on them.

11

Empty, and icons

Empty · one pattern, four places
An empty list is the normal, good state of a finished day — so it gets no illustration, no button, and no encouragement. A single lede line in ink3, centred in the list area, on plain app ground. Capture is already permanently available, so nothing needs to invite it.
Nothing left for today.
The four lines, fixed: today "Nothing left for today." · first launch "Write the first thing." · habits "No habits yet — they'll appear as patterns do." · history "Nothing logged yet."
Icons · geometry, then restraint
22 pt box, 1.75 pt stroke, round cap and join, no fill. Optically centred to the cap height of row, not to its line box. SF Symbols at weight .regular matches this closely enough to use throughout — custom drawing is for the density grid and the window bar only, and those are graphics, not icons.
Colour rule: icons take ink2 or ink3 and never an accent unless the accent's meaning applies — a terracotta icon is claiming to be the primary action. This is the rule most likely to be broken, because tinted icons look lively in isolation and noisy in a list of forty.
Elevation on dark grounds
A shadow cannot lift a card off deep — the ground is already darker than the shadow. v3 solves it with glass without saying so, which makes it the rule: on light grounds elevation is shadow; on dark grounds elevation is material. One modifier that branches on the ground it is given, so no call site has to know.
Motion & haptics
Two durations and one curve, derived from the decisions rather than invented: 140 ms for state change (press, check, chip) and 260 ms for a surface arriving (sheet, screen), both on easeOut(0, 0, 0.2, 1). Under reduced motion both go to zero and the sheet cross-fades.
Haptics carry the reward the streak counter doesn't: completion is a single soft impact, defer and drop are silent, and a ritual window closing is a light notification. Nothing in the app ever buzzes to ask for attention — only to confirm something you did.

13

Rules for the build

Layout
Leading and trailing only — never left or right. The density grid fills from the trailing edge, so setting .layoutDirection to right-to-left reverses it correctly with no special case. Times and counts must render in Arabic-Indic numerals when the locale asks.
No raw values
No hex, no point literal, no .opacity() on a text colour, no Color.white anywhere. Every one of those is a token that exists — and the first three are how a measured palette silently stops being measured.
Voice
No exclamation marks, no praise, no guilt, and never the second person about the past. "Three of five", not "You missed two". The app states what is true and stops.
Still open · not blocking
Widget and Live Activity need an ink-and-glass variant that works on an unknown wallpaper, and the package needs a rule about whether tokens may be removed or only deprecated. Both can be decided after the first screen ships.
Contract summary
Plus the Liquid Glass reconciliation in section 14. 21 colour names with light and dark values each, 6 type roles with leading and text-style anchors bound in, 5 named spaces and 4 radii and a hit constant, 3 elevations that branch on ground, 2 durations and one curve, and 8 components. Everything a screen needs, and nothing a screen doesn't. Section 14 holds the layer rule that governs all of it.
14

Two layers

Glass on action · opaque on content

This is the rule, and it is the one thing in this document v3 gets wrong. Glass belongs to the layer that floats above content — controls, bars, the timer chip. Content stays opaque, because it is the thing being read. Beyond the guideline there is a practical reason: glass samples a region wider than its own bounds and cannot sample other glass, so a list of glass rows inside a glass scroll edge renders badly and costs more the more of it there is.

The one correction · a rename, not a redesign
v3 uses glass for the selected task row, which is content. So glassOn becomes selected — an ordinary opaque fill, which its pure white already effectively was — and the name glass is reserved for the action layer. Its light value is unchanged at pure white; its dark value becomes an opaque #1D4052 instead of white at 18%, because a translucent selected row would still be glass on the content layer. The contract stays at 21 names.

Already aligned · three things
The pill is at 271 uses and the capsule is the native glass shape, so nothing needs redrawing. The measured opaque palette in 01 and 09 is the reduce-transparency fallback, which is normally the hard part of adopting glass. And "two accents, never decorative" is the same instruction as tinting only with semantic intent.
Elevation narrows
Glass carries its own separation, so the blue-black shadows do not apply to the action layer at all. lift and float keep their measured values but narrow to content cards on light grounds, with section 11's rule still holding on dark.
The morph is a gain
Chip expanding into the full timer is exactly what a glass container is for: group the nearby glass so it shares one sampling region, and give the two states a shared identity for the transition. It replaces a custom animation we had not designed, and 260 ms becomes the system's own timing.
The one thing to watch
Section 09's ratios are measured against app and card. On glass the ground is whatever scrolls beneath, so none of them are guaranteed. Rule: type on glass uses ink only — never ink3 — and the chip's label stays label weight 500 so it holds up over a busy list.

Travels unchanged
All 21 colours, the density ramp, the named spaces, the four radii, both elevations, both durations — and section 14's layer rule, which is consistent across iOS, iPadOS, macOS and watchOS. The palette-as-protocol design is what makes this hold.
watchOS needs its own palette
The ground is pure black for OLED, so app, card and deep collapse and section 09's ratios do not survive the substitution — the ink ramp has to be recomputed against black. The density grid is also unreadable at that size and needs a different graphic, not a scaled one. Decide whether watch is in scope before writing it; it is a conformance plus a rethink, not a port.
How to express it
Keep one ThreadsType and one .hit and make their values platform-conditional inside the token, never at the call site. A view that asks for .row should get the right size on every platform without knowing which one it is on.
The Duo correction
Two layouts, not six poses
v3 draws Duo as a fixed 880×660 landscape frame, and a fixed frame is the wrong unit entirely. Apple's guidance is resizability: a compact-width layout for the outer display and a regular-width layout for the inner one are the fundamentals for every pose, and everything in between is the same layout resizing. Adapt by size class, not by orientation — the inner display does not honour an app's supported interface orientations, so an orientation-locked screen simply breaks.
That also corrects something I said earlier. Because the inner display is regular width, it is closer in spirit to an iPad than to a large phone — so the 340 pt sidebar-and-detail layout does belong here, not only on iPad. And the failure Apple explicitly warns about is the one I had proposed: taking a phone screen, making it twice as wide, and letting everything float in the middle of an empty canvas. The extra space should reveal more structure, not more air.
Which leaves the product principle intact, in a sharper form. The second pane is not more tasks — it is the detail of the one task selected: its note and checklist, its session history, the habit's density grid. Structure, not density. Width still buys focus; it just buys it by showing one thing more fully rather than by adding rows.
The guidance offers three ways to spend regular width: split view (two levels of hierarchy at once), reflow (one vertical column becomes two), and sidebar (a tab bar becomes a sidebar, for information-dense apps). Reflow is wrong for a list, and Jamaal is deliberately not information-dense — so split view, with one constraint worth noting: the hierarchy shown must stay identical to the outer display. The inner pane may never introduce a navigation level the phone does not have.
Hits the density grid
Prefer an even column count
The fold is a division region: live only while folded, zero width when flat. But you can query it even when inactive, precisely so a grid can make a stable decision — prefer an even number of columns on a device that has a fold, so it does not reshuffle every time someone bends the phone.
Jamaal cannot take that advice literally: the density grid is seven wide because a week is seven days, and that is semantic. So the rule instead is that the grid never reflows its columns and never spans the division — it lives wholly inside one pane. A month read cut in half by a hinge is worse than a narrow one.
Three reserved regions
The fold (division), and two occlusion regions: the outer camera, always present, which expands into the Dynamic Island for Live Activities — so the timer chip's off-app form lives there — and the inner camera, active only while in use.
Standard bars avoid all of this automatically. Custom ones do not, which is the second time the custom capture bar costs us: it has to claim its space through the reserved-region API rather than assuming an edge is free.
Use a real sheet
Sheets, popovers, context menus and alerts all adapt for free — and a sheet slides away from the fold when the device is partially closed. The settle sheet should therefore be a genuine sheet presentation, not a hand-built overlay, or it forfeits behaviour we would otherwise have to design.
Two things not to do
Never drive layout from the hinge angle — it is for effects only, and layout comes from size classes and regions. And never lock an orientation: the inner display ignores it.
Apple's own summary is the right instruction for us too: move, resize and adapt only when it makes the experience better. For a one-list app that is a very short list of moments.
The pose that earns a screen
Standing · the nightstand
Stood up, the device shows a bedside clock, photos and widgets with StandBy — which is exactly where the Night Planner wants to be. Tomorrow's ritual spine, glanceable, at the moment you are actually going to bed. This is the strongest product fit in the whole device and it needs the unknown-ground ink variant from section 13's open list.
Seated · controls move down
Set down, the controls sit at the bottom for easy access — which suits a bottom-edge capture bar. But this is only true open; see the vertical-bar card, which is the bigger consequence and pulls the other way on the outer display.
The bar goes vertical
The real consequence for Jamaal. On the outer display, navigation bars, toolbars, tab bars and the status bar shift from the top and bottom edges to the right side, reclaiming vertical space and keeping controls under a thumb. On the inner display they stay vertical in landscape and return to horizontal in portrait.
Two catches, and both land on us. Standard containers get this free; custom bars do not — and Jamaal's capture bar is custom. And icon-only controls translate to a vertical bar; text fields do not. A full-width text capture bar has no vertical form, so on the outer display capture must collapse to a single icon control that opens the field, rather than sitting permanently on screen.
Displacement · what moves
As the device partially folds, interactive elements displace away from the fold to stay visible and reachable, while scrolling content is exempt — it already adapts by scrolling. So the task list stays put and the capture bar, chip and sheet buttons move. Move related elements together to keep their relationship; move independent ones alone.
Also: a nano-texture finish reduces glare but diffuses very fine marks, so the 1 px hairline and 3 px density cell are the floor. And authentication is Touch ID in the side button, which matters only if a ritual or note is ever private.
What this costs the build
One token correction — the gutter becomes a per-edge minimum rather than a flat 26, because safe areas on Duo are asymmetric. Otherwise every value in 01 to 04 survives untouched, which is the real test of whether the system was sized right. What changes is layout expressed as size classes rather than frames, a capture control that has a vertical form, and a second pane that shows the selected task in full. Build the compact layout first; the regular layout is the same components with the detail pane revealed, not a redesign.
